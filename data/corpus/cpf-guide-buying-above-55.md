# Looking for your retirement home (members above 55)

## Right-size to meet your retirement needs
If you already own a flat, right-sizing could free up cash from your current flat to boost your retirement savings. A smaller or more affordable home means lower monthly expenses. If your current flat works for you, you may consider housing monetisation schemes such as the Lease Buyback Scheme.

## Type of flats you can go for
- Short-lease 2-room Flexi flats: if you are 55 years old and above, you can buy a short-lease 2-room Flexi flat offered by HDB, with a lease of between 15 and 45 years in 5-year increments, as long as it covers the youngest owner using Ordinary Account savings up to at least age 95.
- Community Care Apartment: if you are 65 years old and above.
- Resale flats: you can also buy any flat in the resale market.

Consider a more affordable flat that protects your retirement savings, and consider living near your family; the Government provides housing grants and schemes to encourage families to live closer together.

## Determining the budget for your flat
If you are selling your current flat to buy another flat, your budget is the sum of: cash proceeds (selling price of current flat - outstanding housing loan - CPF housing refunds); cash savings; OA savings (including refunds from the sale of current flat); housing grant (if eligible). If you are a new homebuyer, your budget is the sum of cash savings, OA savings and housing grant (if eligible).

## Financing tips
- Set aside enough cash for expenses not payable using CPF, including resale levy if you are purchasing a second subsidised flat, your agent's commission and renovation costs.
- Keep in mind the impact on your retirement. Every dollar less you spend from your CPF account on your home is at least a dollar more for your retirement.
- Avoid overstretching your Ordinary Account savings: keep monthly instalments within your Ordinary Account contribution, and consider keeping 6 months of instalments in your OA as a buffer.

## How much CPF savings can be used
CPF savings can be used for the purchase price, housing loan, stamp and legal fees. If you are aged 55 and above and selling your current flat, the CPF refunds will first be used to top up your Retirement Account up to your Full Retirement Sum; the remaining refunds will be retained in your Ordinary Account. If you are buying a resale flat, use the Housing Usage Calculator to estimate the amount of CPF savings you may use based on the flat's remaining lease. If you are buying a short-lease flat such as a 2-room Flexi flat or a Community Care Apartment, you are not eligible for housing loans.

## When should you sell your flat?
Generally, you have up to 6 months to sell your current flat after the purchase completion of your next property. There is no need to sell the current flat first unless you need the sales proceeds to fund the purchase.
