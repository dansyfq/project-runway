# Home buying guide for members below 55 (extracts: resale flats, financing, cash payments)

## What type of flats can you go for? Resale flats
Check: condition of flat; remaining lease of flat and whether it can last the youngest buyer till age 95.

## Wondering where to live next?
Estates are classified into mature or non-mature estates. In general, flats in non-mature estates are more affordable than those in mature estates. However, this phenomenon may change with the times.
Mature Estates: Ang Mo Kio, Bedok, Bishan, Bukit Merah, Bukit Timah, Central Area, Clementi, Geylang, Kallang/Whampoa, Marine Parade, Pasir Ris, Queenstown, Serangoon, Tampines, Toa Payoh.
Non-mature Estates: Bukit Batok, Bukit Panjang, Choa Chu Kang, Hougang, Jurong East, Jurong West, Punggol, Sembawang, Sengkang, Woodlands, Yishun.

## Should you buy or sell your flat first?
If you buy another HDB before selling, you have to sell your current flat within 6 months of purchasing your new flat, and you may have a reduced cash flow. Whether you buy your next flat or sell your existing one first, the Loan-to-Value limits remain the same i.e., up to 75% for both HDB loan and bank loan.

## How much do you need to buy your next home?
When you first purchase your home, you have to make some upfront payments and take a loan to finance the remainder of the payment. There are different ways to finance your home: grants, loans, your Ordinary Account savings and your cash savings, including the sales proceeds from your existing flat. Your Ordinary Account savings can be used for the downpayment and monthly instalments. However, there are limits to the amount of Ordinary Account savings you can use for your property. Use the Housing Usage calculator to estimate the amount. Housing grants obtained are usually credited to the applicant's Ordinary Account after the booking of flat. Housing grants can generally only be used for the downpayment, to offset the purchase price.

## How is your loan quantum determined?
The loan quantum depends on the type of loan you choose to take, as well as your Loan-to-Value amount, total debt servicing ratio, mortgage servicing ratio and loan tenure.
Total debt servicing ratio: the TDSR refers to the portion of a borrower's gross monthly income that goes towards repaying the monthly debt obligations, including the loan being applied for. A borrower's TDSR should be less than or equal to 55%. E.g., if your combined monthly income is $8,000, you can use up to $4,400 (55%) for your total monthly debt repayment. If you have other loan obligations such as a car loan of $1,500, your monthly instalment for your home will be capped at up to $2,900.
Mortgage servicing ratio: the MSR refers to the portion of a borrower's gross monthly income that goes towards repaying property loans. The MSR is capped at 30% of a borrower's gross monthly income. To be prudent, we recommend capping the monthly instalment at 25% of your monthly income. MSR applies only to housing loans for the purchase of a HDB flat, or an executive condominium. E.g., if your combined monthly income is $8,000, the maximum monthly instalment allowed is $2,400 (30%).
Loan-to-Value limit: the LTV limit determines the maximum amount an individual can borrow from a financial institution for a housing loan. The LTV limit depends on the number of outstanding home loans you have and the loan tenure. A lower LTV would mean that members have to finance more of their downpayment using either CPF or cash savings.
Loan tenure: a shorter loan tenure can mean higher monthly instalments but lower interest paid in total. A longer loan tenure may lower your monthly instalment, but you may end up incurring higher interest expenses in total.

## Some payments to be made in cash
Purchase price above market valuation: when a resale flat is sold above HDB's valuation, the price above the market valuation is known as Cash Over Valuation (COV). You cannot use your Ordinary Account monies to pay for the COV and can only use cash. As HDB's valuation is only obtained after the seller and buyer agrees on the price, you may wish to estimate the price carefully and determine your budget before agreeing on the purchase price.
Resale levy: paid when you subsequently purchase a second subsidised flat.
5% downpayment for bank loan: if you are taking up a bank loan, you have to pay 5% of the downpayment in cash. The remaining 20% can be paid using CPF savings.
Miscellaneous costs paid in cash: renovation costs, your agent's commission, option fees.

## Bank loan vs HDB housing loan
- Loan quantum. Bank loan: you can borrow up to 75% of the flat purchase price if the loan period is 25 years and below. HDB loan: you can borrow up to 75% of the flat purchase price; however, you will be required to use all of your Ordinary Account savings above $20,000 first.
- Downpayment. Bank loan: the minimum downpayment is 25% of the purchase price; you may use your Ordinary Account savings, however 5% has to be paid in cash. HDB loan: the minimum downpayment is 20% of the purchase price, which can be fully paid using your Ordinary Account savings.
- Use of CPF and previous sale proceeds. Bank loan: you may use or retain any amount in your Ordinary Account (up to applicable limits). HDB loan: if you are taking a second HDB loan, you and the essential occupier need to use 50% of the cash proceeds from the disposal of the existing or last-owned HDB flat.
- Interest rates. Bank: fluctuate with the economic environment. HDB: 0.1% above the Ordinary Account interest rate, which is currently 2.6%.
- Repayment. Bank: fluctuates with interest rate. HDB: relatively constant. Bank loans may have early and late repayment penalties and cannot be refinanced to an HDB loan. HDB loans have no early repayment penalty, a late payment charge of 7.5% per annum, and the option to refinance to a bank loan.

## Should I use cash or CPF to finance my home?
Using cash means less flexibility for other expenses but unused CPF monies contribute to your retirement sum, and you will not need to make a CPF refund upon sale, so you may receive higher cash proceeds. Using CPF frees up cash but you will need to refund more to your CPF account upon sale (principal plus accrued interest). You can use CPF for the initial purchase of your home and subsequently use cash or a mix of both for your mortgage instalments.

## Steps to buying a resale flat
Search, plan, contract: check eligibility to buy flat and grants available; register intent to buy; search for desired flat; negotiate with seller and request for Option to Purchase (OTP); choose mode of financing; request for value from HDB; exercise OTP.
Application process: submit resale application; endorse resale documents; pay resale fees; obtain approval from HDB.
Completion of sale: attend completion appointment at HDB.
