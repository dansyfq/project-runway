# Refund of CPF savings when you sell or transfer your property

You'll need to refund the CPF savings you used with the accrued interest when you sell the whole property, and when you transfer or sell your share of your property to another party.

## Sale proceeds
Upon the sale or transfer of your property, the sale proceeds will be used to pay off the outstanding housing loan taken and refund the CPF amount you used for your property.

## How much do you need to refund?
You can check the amount to be refunded on the "What Happens If" section on your Home ownership dashboard if you were to sell your property now. The amount that you have to refund is the CPF principal amount you withdrew (P) and the accrued interest (I). If you are aged 55 or above and have pledged your property to set aside your retirement sum, you will also need to refund the pledged amount.

Important note: your required CPF refund is less than your P+I if you were aged 55 or older before 1 January 2013; and have set aside your Full Retirement Sum (FRS) before 1 January 2013. This is because having set aside your FRS before 1 January 2013, you are not required by law to refund the CPF you have used to pay for your property prior to 2013. If you have continued to use your CPF from 1 January 2013, only the principal amount used from 1 January 2013, and the accrued interest need to be refunded when you sell your property.

## What happens after you make the refund?
If you are below 55, your housing refunds will be credited to your Ordinary Account (OA). If you are above 55, your housing refunds will be first used to top up your Retirement Account (RA) to meet your required retirement sum and the balance refunds will remain in your OA. You can use your OA savings to pay for another property or participate in other CPF schemes; keep the savings in your CPF accounts to earn risk-free interest; get higher payouts in retirement by transferring the OA balance to your SA (if below 55) or RA (if 55 and above); or withdraw your CPF savings for immediate retirement needs if you are above age 55.

If you are aged 55 and above, and plan to buy a 3-room or smaller HDB flat, you can use your RA savings in excess of your Basic Retirement Sum for your next property purchase if you purchase the flat within 3 years after selling this property, the new flat costs less than what you sold this property for, and you have not been issued with a CPF LIFE plan.

## When sales proceeds are not enough to cover the refund amount
If you have sold the property at market value but the selling price of your property is not enough to cover the outstanding housing loan and the required CPF housing refund, you will only need to refund the selling price less the outstanding housing loan to your CPF account. You do not need to top up the CPF housing refund shortfall in cash, as long as the property is sold at market value.

Any option money (such as an option fee or option exercise fee) you receive from the buyer in cash is considered part of the selling price and must be refunded to your CPF account before the transaction can be completed.

## Making a voluntary housing refund
To reduce the amount you need to refund when you sell your property, consider making voluntary housing refunds. Voluntary housing refunds allows you to enjoy the attractive interest rates of your CPF savings.
